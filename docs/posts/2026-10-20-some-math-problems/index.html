Problems

Problem 1. Let \(d \leq k \leq n\) be positive integers and \(x \in \R_{\geq 0}^{n \times d}\). Prove that there is \(I \subset \{1, \ldots, n\}\) with \(|I| \leq k\) such that \[\sum_{i \in I} x_{ij} \geq \frac{k - d + 1}{n} \sum_{i = 1}^n x_{ij}\] for all \(j = 1, \ldots, d.\)

Problem 2. Let \(n \geq 1\) be an integer, and \(G\) be an undirected simple graph with at most \(2n - 1\) vertices such that for every vertex \(v\), there is a \(n\)-clique (i.e., a complete subgraph with \(n\) vertices) that doesn’t include \(v\). Show that \(G\) has a \(n+1\)-clique.

Problem 3. Let \(n\) be a positive integer, and \(f : [0, n] \to \R\) be continuous and such that \(f(0) = f(n)\). Show that there are at least \(n\) pairs \(\{x, y\} \subset [0, n]\) with \(x \neq y\), \(f(x) = f(y)\), and \(x - y \in \Z\).

Solutions

Problem 1

Problem. Let \(d \leq k \leq n\) be positive integers and \(x \in \R_{\geq 0}^{n \times d}\). Prove that there is \(I \subset \{1, \ldots, n\}\) with \(|I| \leq k\) such that \[\sum_{i \in I} x_{ij} \geq \frac{k - d + 1}{n} \sum_{i = 1}^n x_{ij}\] for all \(j = 1, \ldots, d.\)

Solution. We can normalize \(\sum_{i = 1}^n x_{ij} = 1\) for all \(j\). Consider the set \[W = \bc{w \in \R^n : 0 \leq w_i \leq 1, \sum_{i = 1}^n w_i \leq k - d + 1, \sum_{i = 1}^n w_i x_{ij} \geq \frac{k - d + 1}{n} \text{ for all } j}.\] Clearly \((\frac{k - d + 1}{n}, \ldots, \frac{k - d + 1}{n}) \in W\), so it’s nonempty. Let \(w \in W\) with the smallest number \(r\) of fractional coordinates \(w_i \not\in \{0, 1\}\).

Consider the \(n - r\) vectors \(e_i\) for \(w_i \in \{0, 1\}\), plus the \(d\) vectors \((x_{ij})_{i=1}^n\) for \(j=1,\ldots,d\). If \(n - r + d \leq n - 1\), i.e., \(r \geq d + 1\), there is \(v \in \R^n\) nonzero that is perpendicular to all these vectors. This means: \(v_i = 0\) if \(w_i \in \{0, 1\}\), \(v_i \neq 0\) for some \(i\), and \(\sum_i v_i x_{ij} = 0\) for all \(j\). Now, \(w + tv \in W\) for \(t \in \R\) close to 0. There is \(\ol t > 0\) minimum, and \(\ul t < 0\) maximum, such that \(w_i + tv_i \in \{0, 1\}\) for some \(i\) with \(w_i\) fractional when \(t = \ol t\) or \(\ul t\). If \(\sum_i v_i \geq 0\), take \(t = \ul t\); otherwise, take \(t = \ol t\). In both cases, \(w + tv \in W\) and it has at most \(r - 1\) fractional coordinates. This contradicts that \(r\) is minimum. Therefore, \(r < d + 1\), i.e., \(r \leq d\).

Take \(I = \{i : w_i > 0\}\). I’ll show that \(|I| \leq k\). If \(r = 0\) then \(|I| = \sum_i w_i \leq k - d + 1 \leq k\). Suppose that \(r \geq 1\). We have \[\sum_{i : w_i = 1} 1 = \sum_{i : w_i \in \{0, 1\}} w_i < \sum_{i : w_i \in \{0, 1\}} w_i + \sum_{i : 0 < w_i < 1} w_i = \sum_i w_i \leq k - d + 1;\] since it’s an integer, \(\sum_{i : w_i = 1} 1 \leq k - d\). Therefore, \(|I| = \sum_{i : w_i = 1} 1 + r \leq k - d + d = k\), as desired.

Now, for each \(j\), \(\sum_{i \in I} x_{ij} \geq \sum_{i} w_i x_{ij} \geq \frac{k - d + 1}{n}\), as desired. \(\blacksquare\)

Comment. This is not always best possible, but it’s best possible whenever \(n = dp\) and \(k = dq - 1\) with \(p \geq q\) positive integers. We have \(\frac{k - d + 1}{n} = \frac{d(q-1)}{dp} = \frac{q-1}{p}\). Take \[x^\top = \overset{\displaystyle \begin{matrix}\overbrace{\hphantom{1 \; \cdots \; 1}}^{p} & \overbrace{\hphantom{1 \; \cdots \; 1}}^{p} & \phantom{\cdots} & \overbrace{\hphantom{1 \; \cdots \; 1}}^{p}\end{matrix}}{\begin{pmatrix} 1 \; \cdots \; 1 & 0 \; \cdots \; 0 & \cdots & 0 \; \cdots \; 0 \\ 0 \; \cdots \; 0 & 1 \; \cdots \; 1 & \cdots & 0 \; \cdots \; 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 \; \cdots \; 0 & 0 \; \cdots \; 0 & \cdots & 1 \; \cdots \; 1 \end{pmatrix}}.\] Suppose that \(I \subset \{1, \ldots, n\}\) with \(|I| \leq k\). We have \(\sum_j \sum_{i \in I} x_{ij} = \sum_{i \in I} \sum_j x_{ij} = \sum_{i \in I} 1 = |I| \leq k\), so there is \(j\) with \(\sum_{i \in I} x_{ij} \leq k/d < q\); since it’s an integer, \(\sum_{i \in I} x_{ij} \leq q - 1 = \frac{q-1}{p} \sum_i x_{ij}\). So \(\frac{k - d + 1}{n}\) is best possible in this case.

Problem 2

Problem. Let \(n \geq 1\) be an integer, and \(G\) be an undirected simple graph with at most \(2n - 1\) vertices such that for every vertex \(v\), there is a \(n\)-clique (i.e., a complete subgraph with \(n\) vertices) that doesn’t include \(v\). Show that \(G\) has a \(n+1\)-clique.

Solution. By induction. The case \(n=1\) is trivial. Suppose \(n \geq 2\). There is an \(n\)-clique \(C_1\). For \(i \geq 1\), until \(C_1 \cap \cdots \cap C_i = \empty\), take \(u \in C_1 \cap \cdots \cap C_i\) and find an \(n\)-clique \(C_{i+1}\) with \(u \not\in C_{i+1}\). We obtain \(n\)-cliques \(C_1, \ldots, C_k\) with \(k \geq 2\), and \(A := C_1 \cap \cdots \cap C_{k-1} \neq \empty\) but \(A \cap C_k = \empty\).

Let \(B := (C_1 \cup \cdots \cup C_{k-1}) \menos A\). If \(B\) has at most \(2(n - |A|) - 1\) vertices then the inductive hypothesis applies, since each vertex in \(B\) is outside of some \(C_i \menos A\), which is a \(n - |A|\)-clique. Therefore, \(B\) has a \(n - |A| + 1\)-clique \(C\). Now, \(A \cup C\) is a \(n+1\)-clique, and we are done.

Suppose that \(|B| \geq 2(n - |A|)\). We have \[ \begin{aligned} 2n - 1 &\geq |A \cup B \cup C_k| \\ &= |A| + |B \cup C_k| \\ &= |A| + |B| + |C_k| - |B \cap C_k| \\ &\geq |A| + 2(n - |A|) + n - |B \cap C_k| \\ &= 3n - |A| - |B \cap C_k|, \end{aligned} \] so \(|A| + |B \cap C_k| \geq n + 1\). But \(A \cup (B \cap C_k)\) is a clique, and we are done. \(\blacksquare\)

Problem 3

Problem. Let \(n\) be a positive integer, and \(f : [0, n] \to \R\) be continuous and such that \(f(0) = f(n)\). Show that there are at least \(n\) pairs \(x, y \in [0, n]\) with \(x \neq y\), \(f(x) = f(y)\), and \(x - y \in \Z\).

Solution. By induction. The case \(n = 1\) is trivial. Suppose that \(n \geq 2\). We have \(0 = f(n) - f(0) = \sum_{k = 0}^{n-1} (f(k+1) - f(k))\), so there is \(k\) such that \(f(k) = f(k+1)\), or \(f(k+1) - f(k)\) and \(f(k+2) - f(k+1)\) have opposite signs; either way, there is \(r \in [k, k+1]\) such that \(f(r+1) = f(r)\).

Take \(g : [0,n-1] \to \R\) given by \(g(x) = f(x)\) for \(x \leq r\) and \(g(x) = f(x+1)\) for \(x>r\). It satisfies the inductive hypothesis, so there are \(n-1\) pairs \(x, y \in [0,n-1]\) with \(x-y\in\Z\), \(x\neq y\) and \(g(x)=g(y)\). By adding \(1\) to numbers in \([r,n-1]\) we put all pairs in \([0,n]\), and they satisfy \(f(x)=f(y)\). Add the pair \(\{r, r+1\}\) and we obtain \(n\) pairs, as desired. \(\blacksquare\)

Problem 4

Problem. Let \(n \geq 2\) an integer, \(X_0 = p \in [0, 1]\) and \(X_{t+1} = B/n\) with \(B | X_t \sim \text{Binomial}(n, X_t)\) for all \(t \geq 0\), and \(\tau = \min\{t \geq 0 : X_t \in \{0, 1\}\}\). Show that \[\E(\tau) = H(p) n + o(n),\] where \(H(p) = -2p \log(p) - 2 (1-p) \log(1-p)\).

Solution. Notice that \(H\) is concave and \(H(0) = H(1) = 0\). The idea is to study \(D(x) = H(x) - \E[H(X)] \geq 0\) by Jensen, where \(X = B/n\), \(B \sim \text{Binomial}(n, x)\). We’ll show that most of the time \(D(X_t) \approx 1/n\), so \(H(p) = \E[H(X_0) - H(X_\tau)] = \E\bs{\sum_{t<\tau} D(X_t)} \approx \E(\tau)/n\) and therefore \(\E(\tau) \approx H(p) n\).
