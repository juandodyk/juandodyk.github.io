Wright–Fisher Fixation Time: The Logarithmic Correction

Published

October 23, 2026

Statement

The Wright–Fisher chain is \(X_0 = p\) and, given \(X_t = x\), \(X_{t+1} = Z/n\) with \(Z \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(n, x)\). Let \(\tau_n = \min\bc{t \geq 1 : X_t \in \{0, 1\}}\) and \(H(x) = -2x\log x - 2(1-x)\log(1-x)\).

Theorem. There is an absolute constant \(C\) such that for all \(n \geq 2\) and \(p \in [0, 1]\), \[\abs{\E_p[\tau_n] - nH(p) + \tfrac13 \log\bp{1 + np(1-p)}} \leq C.\] In particular, \(\E_p[\tau_n] = nH(p) - \frac13 \log n + O(1)\) for fixed \(p \in (0, 1)\).

Numerically, the quantity inside the absolute value lies in \([-0.96, 0.03]\) for \(n \in \{10, 50, 200, 1000, 3000\}\), at every lattice starting point and at several non-lattice ones.

The idea is to work on the lattice with harmonic numbers \(\HH_m = \sum_{j=1}^m \frac1j\) in place of logarithms. Binomial expectations of harmonic numbers have closed forms, so all drifts can be computed exactly up to errors that sum to \(O(1)\).

Tools

On the lattice we write states as integers \(k \in \{0, \ldots, n\}\), with \(x = k/n\), \(q = 1 - x\) and \(Z \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(n, x)\). For a function \(f\) on \(\{0, \ldots, n\}\), let \(\Delta f(k) = f(k) - \E[f(Z)]\) be its expected drop in one step. For \(1 \leq k \leq n - 1\), let \(w = \frac{1}{k^2} + \frac{1}{(n-k)^2}\). Note that \(w \geq \frac{4}{n^2}\).

(T1) Euler–Maclaurin. \(\HH_m = \log m + \gamma + \frac{1}{2m} - e_m\), where \(0 \leq \frac{1}{12m^2} - e_m \leq \frac{1}{120m^4}\).

(T2) Binomial identities. If \(B \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(m, x)\), then \[\E[\HH_B] = \sum_{r=1}^m \frac{1 - q^r}{r}, \qquad \E\bs{\frac{1}{B + 1}} = \frac{1 - q^{m+1}}{(m+1)x}.\] Proof. Write \(\HH_B = \int_0^1 \frac{1 - t^B}{1 - t}\,dt\) and \(\frac{1}{B+1} = \int_0^1 t^B\,dt\), use \(\E[t^B] = (q + xt)^m\), and substitute \(u = q + xt\). \(\square\)

(T3) Size bias. \(\E[Z f(Z)] = k\,\E[f(B + 1)]\) with \(B \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(n - 1, x)\).

(T4) Tails. \(\sum_{r \geq 1} \frac{q^r}{r} = -\log x = \log\frac nk\). For \(N \geq n\), \(\sum_{r \geq N} \frac{q^r}{r} \leq \frac1N \cdot \frac{q^N}{x} \leq \frac{q^n}{k}\). Also \(q^n \leq e^{-k} \leq \frac{1}{k^2}\).

Three drift computations

Fix \(1 \leq k \leq n - 1\).

Lemma 1. Let \(u(k) = k(\HH_n - \HH_k)\). Then \(\Delta u(k) = \frac q2 + k e_k - k e_n + \rho\) with \(\abs\rho \leq e^{-k}\).

Proof. By (T3) and (T2), using \(\sum_{r=1}^{n-1}\frac1r = \HH_n - \frac1n\), \[\E[u(Z)] = k\HH_n - k\E\bs{\HH_B + \tfrac{1}{B+1}} = x + k\sum_{r=1}^{n-1}\frac{q^r}{r} - 1 + q^n.\] By (T4), \(\sum_{r=1}^{n-1} \frac{q^r}{r} = \log\frac nk - T\) with \(0 \leq kT \leq q^n\). By (T1), \(\HH_n - \HH_k - \log\frac nk = \frac{1}{2n} - \frac{1}{2k} - e_n + e_k\). Therefore \[\Delta u(k) = k\bp{\tfrac{1}{2n} - \tfrac{1}{2k} - e_n + e_k} + 1 - x + (kT - q^n) = \tfrac q2 + ke_k - ke_n + (kT - q^n). \qquad \square\]

Lemma 2. \(\Delta \HH_k = \frac{1}{2k} - \frac{1}{2n} - e_k + e_n - \rho'\) with \(0 \leq \rho' \leq \frac{e^{-k}}{k}\).

Proof. By (T2) and (T4), \(\E[\HH_Z] = \HH_n - \sum_{r=1}^n \frac{q^r}{r} = \HH_n - \log\frac nk + \rho'\) with \(\rho' = \sum_{r > n} \frac{q^r}{r}\). Now apply (T1) to \(\HH_k - \HH_n + \log\frac nk\). \(\square\)

Lemma 3. \(\Delta\bs{-\frac{1}{k+1}} \geq \frac{q}{12k^2}\).

Proof. By (T2) with \(m = n\), \[\E\bs{\frac{1}{Z+1}} - \frac{1}{k+1} = \frac{q\bp{1 - (k+1)q^n}}{(n+1)x(k+1)} = \frac{n}{n+1} \cdot \frac{q\bp{1 - (k+1)q^n}}{k(k+1)}.\] Since \((k+1)q^n \leq (k+1)e^{-k} \leq \frac2e\), \(\frac{n}{n+1} \geq \frac23\) and \(k + 1 \leq 2k\), this is at least \(\frac{(1 - 2/e)\,q}{3k^2} \geq \frac{q}{12k^2}\). \(\square\)

The test functions

Define, on \(\{0, \ldots, n\}\), \[G(k) = 2u(k) + 2u(n-k) - \tfrac13\bp{\HH_k + \HH_{n-k}}, \qquad \varphi(k) = -\frac{1}{k+1} - \frac{1}{n-k+1}.\] Both are symmetric under \(k \leftrightarrow n - k\). Since \(Z \leftrightarrow n - Z\) swaps \(x\) and \(q\), the drift of \(u(n - k)\) is given by Lemma 1 with \(x\) and \(q\) swapped, and similarly for \(\HH_{n-k}\).

Claim. For \(1 \leq k \leq n - 1\), \[\abs{\Delta G(k) - 1 - \tfrac{1}{6n}} \leq 3w, \qquad \Delta\varphi(k) \geq \tfrac{w}{48}.\]

Proof. Add up Lemmas 1 and 2. The main terms are \(2 \cdot \frac q2 + 2 \cdot \frac x2 = 1\) and \(-\frac13\bp{\frac{1}{2k} + \frac{1}{2(n-k)} - \frac1n}\). The \(e_n\) terms from Lemma 1 combine to \(-2ne_n\). Grouping the rest, \[\Delta G - 1 - \tfrac{1}{6n} = \bs{2ke_k - \tfrac{1}{6k}} + \bs{2(n-k)e_{n-k} - \tfrac{1}{6(n-k)}} + \bs{\tfrac{1}{6n} - 2ne_n} + \tfrac13\bp{e_k + e_{n-k} - 2e_n + \rho'_k + \rho'_{n-k}} + 2\rho_k + 2\rho_{n-k}.\] By (T1) the first three brackets are at most \(\frac{1}{60k^3}\), \(\frac{1}{60(n-k)^3}\) and \(\frac{1}{60n^3}\) in absolute value. The rest is at most \(\frac{w}{36} + \frac{1}{18n^2} + \frac w3 + 2w\) by (T1) and (T4). The total is below \(3w\).

For \(\varphi\), Lemma 3 and its mirror give \(\Delta\varphi \geq \frac{1}{12}\bp{\frac{q}{k^2} + \frac{x}{(n-k)^2}}\). By symmetry, take \(k \leq \frac n2\). Then \(q \geq \frac12\) and \(\frac{1}{k^2} \geq \frac w2\), so \(\Delta\varphi \geq \frac{w}{48}\). \(\square\)

From drifts to the expected time

Let \(\sigma = \min\bc{t \geq 0 : X_t \in \{0, 1\}}\). From every interior state the chain is absorbed in one step with probability at least \(n^{-n}\), so \(\E_k[\sigma] < \infty\). For every function \(f\) on the lattice, optional stopping gives \[f(k) - \E_k[f(X_\sigma)] = \E_k\bs{\sum_{t < \sigma} \Delta f(nX_t)}.\]

Let \(F_\pm = G \pm 144\varphi\). By the Claim, \(\Delta F_+ \geq 1 + \frac{1}{6n}\) and \(\Delta F_- \leq 1 + \frac{1}{6n}\) at every interior state. Since \(F_\pm(0) = F_\pm(n)\), \[F_-(k) - F_-(0) \leq \bp{1 + \tfrac{1}{6n}}\E_k[\sigma] \leq F_+(k) - F_+(0).\] Since \(0 \leq \varphi(k) - \varphi(0) \leq \frac32\), \[\abs{\bp{1 + \tfrac{1}{6n}}\E_k[\sigma] - G(k) + G(0)} \leq 216.\]

Now we evaluate \(G(k) - G(0)\). Since \(u(0) = u(n) = 0\), we have \(G(0) = -\frac13\HH_n\). For \(1 \leq k \leq n - 1\), (T1) gives \[2u(k) + 2u(n-k) = nH(x) - 1 + 2ke_k + 2(n-k)e_{n-k} - 2ne_n = nH(x) + O(1),\] \[\HH_k + \HH_{n-k} - \HH_n = \log\frac{k(n-k)}{n} + O(1) = \log(1 + nxq) + O(1).\] For the second line, note that \(nxq \geq \frac12\). Hence \(G(k) - G(0) = nH(x) - \frac13\log(1 + nxq) + O(1)\). In particular \(\abs{G(k) - G(0)} = O(n)\), so dividing by \(1 + \frac{1}{6n}\) costs only \(O(1)\). Therefore, for every lattice state \(x = k/n\) (the cases \(k = 0, n\) being trivial), \[\E_x[\sigma] = nH(x) - \tfrac13\log(1 + nxq) + O(1).\]

Arbitrary starting point

Since the first step lands on the lattice, \[\E_p[\tau_n] = 1 + n\E_p[H(X_1)] - \tfrac13\E_p\bs{\log\bp{1 + nX_1(1 - X_1)}} + O(1).\] It remains to replace \(X_1\) by \(p\) in both terms, at cost \(O(1)\). By symmetry, assume \(p \leq \frac12\), and write \(q = 1 - p\).

The entropy term. Concavity of \(H\) and \(\E_p[X_1] = p\) give \(\E_p[H(X_1)] \leq H(p)\). Conversely, the size-bias identity (T3) with \(f(j) = \log\frac jn\), together with its mirror image, gives \[\tfrac12\bp{H(p) - \E_p[H(X_1)]} = p\,\E\bs{\log\frac{B+1}{np}} + q\,\E\bs{\log\frac{B'+1}{nq}},\] with \(B \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(n-1, p)\) and \(B' \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(n-1, q)\). By concavity of \(\log\), the first term is at most \(p\log\bp{1 + \frac{q}{np}} \leq \frac qn\), and the second is at most \(\frac pn\). Hence \(nH(p) - 2 \leq n\E_p[H(X_1)] \leq nH(p)\).

The logarithmic term. Since \(y \mapsto \log(1 + ny)\) is concave and \(\E_p[X_1(1-X_1)] = (1 - \frac1n)pq\), Jensen gives the upper bound \(\log(1 + npq)\).

For the lower bound, if \(np \leq 8\) there is nothing to prove, since then \(\log(1 + npq) \leq \log 9\) and the expectation is nonnegative. Otherwise, let \(A = \bc{\abs{X_1 - p} \leq \frac p2}\). By Chebyshev, \(\Pr(A^c) \leq \frac{4pq}{np^2} \leq \frac{4}{np}\). On \(A\), \(X_1 \geq \frac p2\) and \(1 - X_1 \geq \frac14 \geq \frac q4\), so \(1 + nX_1(1 - X_1) \geq \frac18(1 + npq)\). Therefore \[\E_p\bs{\log\bp{1 + nX_1(1-X_1)}} \geq \log(1 + npq) - \log 8 - \frac{4\log(1 + np)}{np} \geq \log(1 + npq) - \log 8 - 4.\] This completes the proof. \(\blacksquare\)

Remarks

  • Where the \(\frac13\) comes from. It is the Euler–Maclaurin coefficient \(\frac1{12}\): Lemma 1 gives an excess drift \(2ke_k \approx \frac{1}{6k}\) at state \(k\). That excess is cancelled by \(\frac13\) of the harmonic-number correction, whose drift is \(\frac{1}{2k}\). The correction itself changes by \(\frac13\HH_n \approx \frac13 \log n\) between the middle and the boundary.
  • The other ingredients are bounded. The term \(\varphi\) only absorbs errors of order \(\frac{1}{k^2}\), and its range is bounded. The \(\frac{1}{6n}\) in the drift changes \(\E\sigma = O(n)\) by \(O(1)\).
  • Constants. The constants (\(144\), \(216\), and the \(O(1)\) terms) are far from optimal and were chosen for convenience.
